This week's companies

Three fictional companies are under consideration this week.

Perigee House

ProductA four-bed expandable module in low Earth orbit. Guests stay 5 to 7 days, then fly home.
TransportPerigee owns no crew vehicle. It buys whole flights from one crew transport provider. Each flight carries four people.
HardwareA full-size module has passed pressure tests on the ground, and a 30-day crew test in a ground mockup is complete. Nothing has flown. The automated systems for a stay with no crew aboard have been tested only in computer simulation.
FundingRaising money now to build and launch the module.

Hexpave Lunar

Founded2024, U.S. Raised a $40M Series A from a space VC fund and a construction-materials company.
ProductSix autonomous rovers, about 150 kg each. They sinter regolith into hexagonal pavers with microwaves and lay pads, berms and roads.
OperationsSupervised autonomy from Earth, with a round-trip delay of about 2.5 seconds.
CustomersOne-Sixth Park (anchor), a NASA Moon Base pad bid, lander companies.

One-Sixth Park

FoundedU.S. Media and theme-park veterans, backed by VC, a gaming studio and brand sponsors.
SiteA flat near-side mare plain in view of Earth, about 40 km from the nearest Apollo landing site.
OwnsNo robots. Hires Hexpave and buys lander rides.
Revenue nowSubscribers on Earth drive a real rover, plus livestreams, gaming licenses and naming rights. Crewed visitors come later.

How the three companies connect

Same module, two worlds

One-Sixth Park's worker quarters use the same expandable module that Perigee House flies in orbit. Perigee's first flight is the module's first test in space.

Orbit first, Moon later

Perigee House's guests are One-Sixth Park's most likely future visitors. People who pay for a week in orbit are the best prospects for a trip to the Moon, so Perigee's bookings are the first real evidence of demand for crewed lunar visits.

IN CLASS
In orbit

Perigee House: a hotel in orbit

Perigee House wants to run the first hotel in low Earth orbit. Before your firm invests, Perigee has to decide how a stay will run: with only guests aboard, or with its own crew.

Two ways to run a stay

Model A: Autonomous

No Perigee crew aboard. The crew vehicle flies itself, and the module runs on automated systems watched by mission control on the ground. All four seats go to paying guests.

Model B: Crewed

One or two Perigee crew members fly up with each group and stay the whole visit: a commander, and if there are two, a host who is also a flight engineer. That leaves three seats for guests, or two.

Both have precedent. Crew Dragon is designed to fly itself, and Inspiration4 (2021) carried four private crew with no professional astronaut aboard. NASA requires private missions to the ISS to have an experienced former astronaut as commander, which is why every Axiom flight gives up one seat.

On the Moon

On the Moon: Hexpave Lunar and One-Sixth Park

Hexpave Lunar builds infrastructure with robots. One-Sixth Park hires it to build the first amusement park on the Moon. Both depend on what Perigee House proves in orbit.

The contract and the build

A $120M fixed-price, milestone-based contract, modeled on NASA's CLPS approach: 20% at signing, then payments only when each structure passes an acceptance test on the Moon. Hexpave may sell pads to other customers. One-Sixth Park buys the lander rides, and buys its habitat module from Perigee House. Each company insures its own hardware.

  1. 1

    Landing pad

    2028

    Rovers grade the site and lay a 30 m pad with a berm, so every later lander touches down on a hard surface.

  2. 2

    Worker quarters

    2029–2030

    A second lander delivers an expandable module built on Earth to Perigee House's design. Hexpave's robots build a vaulted shell of sintered pavers over it, leaving a gap so no weight rests on the module. The shell protects against radiation, micrometeoroids and the temperature swings of the lunar day and night. At first the workers are mostly robots: the quarters include a bay to charge, repair and survive the two-week lunar night.

  3. 3

    The park

    2031 onward

    Unpressurized attractions first, such as a low-gravity jump zone and a rover race track. Pressurized rides and crewed visitors only when transport allows.

Both companies and every figure here are fictional, for teaching.
